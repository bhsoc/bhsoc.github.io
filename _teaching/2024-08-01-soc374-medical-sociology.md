Independently designed and taught an upper-level Medical Sociology course enrolling 46 students from Sociology, Human Development and Family Studies, Public Health, and pre-medical programs. Students used foundational sociological concepts to evaluate competing explanations of health and illness, culminating in a critical reflection paper and a Local Community Health Disparity Project in which student teams investigated a local health disparity and its social determinants. Most evaluation items exceeded 4.60 on a five-point scale.
