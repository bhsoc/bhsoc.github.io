I am a medical sociologist and quantitative methodologist studying how social structures and social judgments produce health inequalities across the life course. I am currently a Postdoctoral Scholar at the Population Research Institute at The Pennsylvania State University, where I work on an NIH-funded project examining educational disparities in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias. I received my dual-title Ph.D. in Sociology and Gerontology from Purdue University in 2025.

My research asks how seemingly individual experiences of health are socially produced and socially evaluated. One line of my work examines when health behaviors become bases of stigma and social judgment, including how judgments vary across behaviors and social groups. A second examines how gender and social relationships organize health practices and inequalities, particularly within intimate relationships and across the life course. My work on aging asks how social inequality accumulates into disparities in cognitive health and how age and health become intertwined in the ways people perceive and treat older adults. Across these projects, I am interested in connecting micro-level social processes with broader patterns of health inequality.

Methodologically, my work lies at the intersection of quantitative sociology, social psychology, and computational social science. I use survey and factorial experiments, longitudinal and population-based data, latent-variable and multilevel models, causal and predictive methods, and computational approaches. I am particularly interested in developing and applying methods that make complex social processes more interpretable rather than treating methodological innovation as an end in itself.
