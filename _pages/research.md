My research examines how social judgments and social structures produce health inequalities across the life course. One line of work asks when and why everyday health behaviors become bases of stigma; a second examines how gender and social relationships organize health practices and inequalities, particularly within intimate relationships; and a third investigates how social inequality accumulates into disparities in cognitive health and aging. Methodologically, I combine survey and factorial experiments, longitudinal and population-based data, and computational and statistical methods to connect individual-level social processes with population-level patterns of health inequality. For details, see my [Publications](/publications/).
