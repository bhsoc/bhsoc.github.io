I am a medical sociologist and quantitative methodologist studying how social structures and social judgments produce health inequalities across the life course. My work follows three connected lines of research, summarized below. For full citations, abstracts, and links, see my [Publications](/publications/) page.

## How Do Social Judgments and Structures Shape Health Behaviors and Outcomes?

My first line of research develops a comparative, multidimensional account of how common health behaviors become bases of stigma and moral classification. My dissertation draws on an original factorial survey experiment I designed and fielded, yielding 10,744 vignette evaluations from 1,792 U.S. adults, and received the [American Sociological Association Sociology of Mental Health Section's Best Dissertation Award](/cv/). Three sole-authored papers examine distinct dimensions of health behavior stigma: how stigma depends on the type of norm violated ([Han, under review](/publication/han-underreview-smh)), how penalties accumulate configurationally across multiple behaviors ([Han, forthcoming](/publication/han-forthcoming-jhsb)), and how age and gender modify — or fail to modify — these judgments ([Han, under review](/publication/han-underreview-sf)). I extend this experimental approach to racial and ethnic inequality in leadership perceptions in a co-first-authored study of multiracial churches ([Hu and Han 2025](/publication/hu-han-2025-ssr)).

I also examine how health practices and outcomes are organized through gender and social relationships. Using Add Health, I combine a machine-learning measure of gender-typical behavior with latent class analysis to identify six health lifestyles and show that gender identity, presentation, and behavior have distinct associations with health practices ([Han and Mize 2026](/publication/han-mize-2026-ssr)). Two qualitative studies drawing on interviews with Chinese migrant workers extend this relational perspective to contraception ([Kane, Han, and Chung 2024](/publication/kane-han-chung-2024-plos-one); [Han, Kane, and Chung 2026](/publication/han-kane-chung-2026-ssmqr)), and a study under review at *Demography* uses longitudinal PSID data and a graph convolutional network to estimate spousal interdependence in body weight ([Han et al., under review](/publication/han-shen-underreview-demography)).

## How Do Social Contexts Shape Health and Aging Across the Life Course?

My second line of research examines how social and physical contexts shape cognitive and emotional well-being across the life course. With Ken Ferraro, I developed the concept of *cognitive capital* — cognitively enriching neighborhood resources — and show that older adults in higher-cognitive-capital neighborhoods have higher cognitive functioning ([Ferraro and Han 2025](/publication/ferraro-han-2025-innovation-in-aging)). A separate study of 769 older adults across greenways in Taichung, Taiwan shows that denser vegetation is associated with greater well-being, especially among those with stronger place attachment ([Han, Li, and Chang 2021](/publication/han-li-chang-2021-ufug)). I also study gendered patterns in depressive symptoms and subjective age in midlife, and collaborate on research examining sex differences in dementia prevalence in China and Taiwan and the mental health effects of caregiving among co-residing older couples.

## How Can Methodological Innovation Advance Research on Health Inequality?

With Trenton Mize, I introduced marginal-effect inequality and total marginal effect summary measures — two model-agnostic tools for summarizing the effects of categorical and ordinal variables ([Mize and Han 2025](/publication/mize-han-2025-sociological-science)). I led development of the publicly available Stata commands `meinequality` and `totalme`, which make these methods accessible to applied researchers, with a companion software article under review at *The Stata Journal* ([Han and Mize, under review](/publication/han-mize-underreview-stata-journal)). A forthcoming handbook chapter extends this framework across linear and nonlinear models ([Mize and Han, forthcoming](/publication/mize-han-forthcoming-handbook)).

## Future Research: How Does Ageism Shape Social Connectedness and Cognitive Aging?

I am building a new stream of research on ageism, social connectedness, and cognitive aging, tracing how ageism is produced in interpersonal interaction, internalized by older adults, and potentially reproduced through emerging technologies. This includes my current postdoctoral work with Liying Luo on an NIH-funded R01 project on educational disparities in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias, and a National Institute on Aging K99/R00 proposal (planned submission: October 2026) examining age-based social exclusion through a survey experiment, a study of internalized ageism and relational withdrawal, and a preregistered audit of whether AI systems reproduce, amplify, or attenuate human age-linked patterns.
